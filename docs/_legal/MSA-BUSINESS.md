## 1. Application and how a contract is made

These terms (the "business terms") apply only where the client is acting for purposes relating to its trade, business, craft or profession. If the client is an individual acting wholly or mainly outside those purposes, our Client Terms for Consumers apply instead, and nothing in these business terms reduces any right the client has as a consumer.

Gridsmith Design, Gridsmith Digital and Gridsmith Press are trading divisions of Gridsmith Ltd and are not separate companies. Every contract is with Gridsmith Ltd ("we" or "us").

Each project is described in a written quotation, proposal, statement of work or similar document (the "Scope"). Before the client accepts a Scope, we provide these business terms in a form the client can store and print, and the Scope names the version that applies.

A contract for a project is formed when the client accepts a Scope that refers to these business terms, by signing it (including electronically) or by confirming acceptance in writing (email is enough). We do not treat payment on its own, or silence, as acceptance, and we do not ask for payment until the Scope has been accepted.

The contract consists of these business terms, the accepted Scope and any accepted Change Order. No other terms apply, including terms in the client's purchase order, supplier portal or standard conditions, even if referred to later, unless we agree to them in a document that names them and is signed on behalf of Gridsmith Ltd.

If documents conflict, an accepted Change Order prevails over the Scope for the matters it changes, and the Scope prevails over these business terms only where the Scope expressly names the clause it changes. Otherwise these business terms prevail.

Where a project is agreed and paid through an online marketplace, that marketplace's terms prevail over these business terms where the parties cannot vary them.

A later version of these business terms does not apply to an existing contract unless both parties agree in writing.

## 2. Scope

We are not required to do work beyond the accepted Scope.

The Scope sets out, as relevant:

- the deliverables;
- assumptions and exclusions;
- the timetable;
- what the client needs to provide, and when;
- the number of revision rounds;
- the price, the payment arrangement and any third-party costs;
- how long the Scope remains open for acceptance;
- any acceptance criteria; and
- any project-specific terms.

If the Scope does not state a payment schedule, we invoice the price for each stage when that stage is delivered, or monthly in arrears for work charged by time. In that case, where the client is an individual, a partnership or another unincorporated body, we instead invoice each stage, each month of work charged by time and any price added by a Change Order before that work starts, and any third-party cost before we commit to it, and payment is due before the work starts or the cost is committed (rather than within the 14 days in clause 5), so that no payment falls due after the work it pays for. For work charged by time, the payment for a month is based on the Scope's estimate for that month or, if it gives none, on the time we propose in writing; work in that month is limited to the time paid for unless more is paid for in advance; and any amount paid for time not used is credited to the next payment or, if there is none, refunded within 14 days of the end of the Scope. The timetable moves while we wait for a payment due under this paragraph, and waiting for it is not a suspension under clause 5. If the Scope does not state a revision allowance, it includes one round of reasonable revisions for each deliverable. A revision round is one consolidated set of the client's comments on a draft, which we then work into the next version.

## 3. Client responsibilities

The client will provide the materials, information, access, decisions, approvals and feedback we reasonably need, when we need them.

The client confirms that it has the right to let us use the materials it gives us for the project.

If the client does not provide something we reasonably need, the timetable moves by at least the length of the delay, and we are not responsible for the resulting delay.

If the project cannot progress for more than 60 days because we are waiting for the client, we may invoice for work done so far under clause 6. If the client still has not responded 14 days after we give written notice, we may treat the project as cancelled by the client under clause 6.

Additional costs caused by client delay are payable only at rates stated in the Scope or agreed in a Change Order.

## 4. Changes and additional work

Reasonable changes within the revision allowance in the Scope are included in the price.

A material change includes a change in direction, deliverables, technical requirements, quantity, platform, approved concept or any other requirement that materially increases the work. We are not required to carry out a material change unless it is agreed.

For a material change we issue a Change Order or additional quotation stating its effect on the price and the timetable. Work on the change begins after the client accepts it by signing it or confirming acceptance in writing, including by email.

If the parties disagree whether a request is within the Scope, we will continue the work that is clearly within the Scope while we discuss it, and we will not charge for the disputed work unless the client agrees in writing.

## 5. Price, invoices and payment

The price and the payment arrangement for each project are set out in its Scope. The arrangement may be payment in full in advance, an advance payment followed by a balance, payments on reaching stated milestones, instalments, a periodic retainer, or another schedule we agree. Any payment made before the related work is done is a payment on account of the price of that work, and clause 6 governs any refund. Where the client is an individual, a partnership or another unincorporated body and any payment falls due after the work it pays for has been supplied, there will be no more than twelve such payments, all due within the 12 months beginning with the date of the contract, with no interest or fee for paying that way.

The Scope states whether VAT is added to the price. No VAT is added to a price unless the Scope or the invoice says so and VAT is legally chargeable.

Unless the Scope or clause 2 says otherwise, prices are in pounds sterling and invoices are payable within 14 days of the invoice date. Payments are made in full, without set-off or deduction except as the law requires, and each party bears its own bank charges.

The client must tell us about any disputed invoice item promptly after receiving the invoice, with its reasons, and must pay the undisputed part by the due date.

If an undisputed amount is overdue, we may suspend work after giving at least 7 days' written notice. The timetable then moves by the period of suspension plus a reasonable restart period, and we are not responsible for the resulting delay. This clause applies subject to any legal restriction on suspending supply to a client in an insolvency procedure.

Late payment may carry statutory interest and compensation under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 where that Act applies. At the date of these business terms that means interest at 8% a year above the Bank of England base rate in force on the 30 June or 31 December before interest starts to run, a fixed sum of £40, £70 or £100 depending on the size of the debt, and reasonable recovery costs above that sum.

## 6. Cancellation by the client

The client may end a project at any time by written notice.

### 6.1 Before work starts

Work starts on the start date in the Scope or, if there is none, when we tell the client in writing that it has begun. Discussing, quoting and preparing the Scope are not work.

If the client cancels before we have started work, we refund all payments received for the project, less any third-party cost that we committed to with the client's prior approval and cannot cancel or recover.

### 6.2 After work starts

If the client cancels after work has started, the client pays:

1. the Scope price of every stage or milestone completed before the cancellation takes effect;
2. for a stage in progress, a fair proportion of that stage's price reflecting the work actually done, measured by time reasonably spent at the rate stated in the Scope where the Scope gives a day or hourly rate, but never more than that stage's price; and
3. third-party costs that we committed to with the client's prior approval and cannot cancel or recover, less anything we do recover.

The total under 1 to 3 never exceeds the total Scope price. We will show how we calculated it on request and refund any amount paid above it within 14 days.

We will deliver the work the client has paid for under this clause in its current state, and clause 9.3 applies to it once paid.

No payment is non-refundable merely because it was paid in advance. A Scope may include a reservation fee for holding a start date only if it states the amount, what it covers and when it is refundable.

## 7. Termination and suspension by Gridsmith

To the extent the law allows, we may end or suspend a project by written notice if the client:

- materially breaches the contract and does not remedy the breach within 14 days of our written notice;
- repeatedly fails to provide information, access, approvals or payments needed to continue, and does not put this right within 14 days of our written notice;
- asks us to do something unlawful; or
- is unable to pay its debts or enters an insolvency procedure.

Where we end a project under this clause, clause 6.2 applies as if the client had cancelled.

We may also end a project for our own reasons by giving 14 days' written notice. If we do, we will deliver, and transfer under clause 9.3, the completed work the client has paid for, refund any amount paid for work not done, and give reasonable help to hand the work over to another supplier.

When a project ends for any reason, amounts due for work done remain payable, and we will return or delete client materials on request, except copies we must keep by law or that are held in routine backups until they are overwritten.

## 8. Delivery, review and acceptance

We deliver in accordance with the Scope.

The client should review each deliverable promptly and tell us of any material non-conformity with the Scope within 10 working days of delivery. We will correct a properly notified non-conformity at no additional charge.

If the client has not identified a material non-conformity within 10 working days of delivery, we may send a written reminder. If there is no response within a further 5 working days, the deliverable is treated as accepted for the purposes of invoicing and moving to the next stage only. This does not affect the client's rights for defects that could not reasonably have been found on review, or our obligations under clause 15.

## 9. Intellectual property

### 9.1 Client materials

Materials the client provides remain the client's property. The client grants us a licence to use them for the project only.

### 9.2 Background material

We keep ownership of our pre-existing or independently developed tools, frameworks, templates, methods, know-how and reusable components ("background material").

Where background material forms part of a deliverable, we grant the client, from payment for that deliverable, a perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive, royalty-free, worldwide licence to use, copy, modify and maintain it as part of that deliverable and works derived from it, and to let the client's contractors do so for the client. The licence passes with the deliverable if the client transfers it. It does not allow use of the background material separately from the deliverable.

### 9.3 Final deliverables

When the client has paid all sums due for a deliverable, we assign to the client all copyright, design rights, database rights and other intellectual property rights we own in the final deliverables created specifically for the project, other than background material and third-party material. When the client has paid in full, we will confirm the assignment in a short document signed, including electronically, on behalf of Gridsmith Ltd, and each party will provide any other document reasonably needed to give effect to it.

Before full payment, the client may use deliverables to review them and, once a stage has been paid, use that stage's deliverables for the purpose stated in the Scope. A genuine dispute about part of a payment does not stop the client using work it has paid for.

Where a subcontractor or other individual contributes to a deliverable, we obtain from them, before their work is used, a written transfer of their rights to us so that this clause can take effect. Where the Scope requires it, we also obtain a written waiver of their moral rights or a written promise not to assert them.

Unless the Scope says otherwise, unselected concepts, drafts and alternatives remain ours, but we will not use them for anyone else in a form that reproduces the client's name, branding or confidential information. Native and working files are supplied only where the Scope lists them. For software and websites, the source code written specifically for the project is a deliverable.

We will tell the client in the Scope or on delivery if we have used generative AI tools to create any part of a deliverable. The law on whether material created with AI tools is protected by copyright is unsettled, so we do not promise that such elements are protected, but we assign any rights that do exist. We review AI-assisted work with the same care as any other work, and we will not put the client's confidential information into an AI tool that uses inputs to train its models without the client's agreement.

Open-source, font, stock, platform and other third-party components remain under their own licences. We will identify any that impose obligations on the client and will not use a licence that would require the client to release its own confidential source code unless the Scope says we may.

### 9.4 Portfolio and publicity

We will not name the client, describe the project or show any work created for the client in our portfolio, website, social media, proposals or marketing unless the client has given its express written consent. Consent may be limited to particular work, uses or timing, and the client may withdraw it for future use at any time by telling us in writing. Without consent, the engagement and its deliverables are treated as the client's confidential information.

### 9.5 Third-party rights

We warrant that, so far as we know after reasonable care, the bespoke deliverables and the materials we choose to include in them do not infringe anyone else's intellectual property rights, and that we hold the licences we say we hold. If a deliverable is found to infringe, we will at our cost modify or replace it, or obtain the licence needed. This clause does not apply to materials the client provides or chooses.

## 10. Confidentiality

Confidential information includes anything marked as confidential and anything a reasonable person would understand to be confidential. Each party may share the other's confidential information with its employees, subcontractors and professional advisers who need it for the project and are bound by duties of confidence at least as strict as this clause.

This clause does not apply to information that is public other than through a breach of this clause, was lawfully known already, is independently developed, or must be disclosed by law.

This clause continues for 5 years after the project ends, and indefinitely for trade secrets and personal data.

## 12. Technical drawing and documentation services

Gridsmith Design's technical services are limited to preparing drawings, technical illustrations, drafting and documentation to the client's brief and from information the client or its advisers provide, as described in the Scope.

We do not provide, and no Scope can add: engineering design or calculations; structural, fire, building-services or other specialist design; certification, approval, stamping or sign-off of any design or drawing; or the role of designer, principal designer or other person responsible for a design under any law or regulation. Where the work needs any of these, the client must appoint an appropriately qualified professional.

We do not accept work consisting of drawings or design details for construction work, a structure or building work within the meaning of construction health-and-safety or building-regulation legislation.

We will perform the technical services with reasonable care and skill. We will rely on the dimensions and information supplied unless the Scope asks us to check them, and we will tell the client if something supplied appears clearly wrong or incomplete.

Drawings marked as draft, preliminary, for comment or superseded must not be used for manufacture, installation or approval. Only drawings issued as final under the Scope are deliverables.

Nothing in this clause limits any responsibility that the law does not allow us to limit, including any duty the law places on us directly.

## 23. Data Processing Schedule

This schedule applies where we process personal data on the client's behalf as its processor under clause 11. In it, "data protection law" means the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, and terms used in that law have the same meaning here.

We will:

1. process the personal data only on the client's documented instructions, including in relation to transfers outside the United Kingdom, unless the law requires otherwise, in which case we will tell the client before processing unless the law prohibits that;
2. ensure that everyone authorised to process the personal data is bound by a duty of confidentiality;
3. take the security measures required by Article 32 of the UK GDPR;
4. not engage another processor without the client's authorisation; the client authorises the sub-processors named in the Scope, we will give at least 14 days' notice of any addition or replacement so the client can object, and if the client objects either party may end the affected services without charge; and we will impose the same data protection obligations on each sub-processor and remain responsible to the client for them;

6. help the client meet its obligations on security, breach notification, data protection impact assessments and prior consultation, taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to us;
7. tell the client without undue delay after becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting the client's personal data;
8. at the client's choice, delete or return the personal data at the end of the services, and delete existing copies unless the law requires us to keep them;
9. make available the information needed to demonstrate compliance with this schedule, and allow for and contribute to audits and inspections by the client or an auditor it appoints, on reasonable notice; and
10. tell the client immediately if, in our opinion, an instruction infringes data protection law.
