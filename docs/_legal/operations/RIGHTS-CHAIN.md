# Rights chain — copyright, assignment and moral rights (P-2)

**Status:** operational requirement adopted at `GS-LEGAL-001-R3` (7 October 2026), replacing the
one-line P-2 in `research/GS-LEGAL-001/R2-OWNER-DECISION-PACK.md` §4.3. This is not legal advice, and
nothing here is a signed document. **No waiver, assignment or contract listed below exists yet.** Each
must be prepared and signed before the first project that relies on it.

**Why it matters.** The client terms promise three things that Gridsmith can deliver only if it holds the
rights first:

| Promise | Where |
|---|---|
| Transfer of rights in final deliverables, with a signed document | Cons 13; Bus 9.3 |
| A written transfer from every subcontractor or other contributor before their work is used | Cons 13 ¶4; Bus 9.3 ¶3 |
| A signed waiver from each writer on a ghostwritten book | Cons 10.2; Bus 13 |

Gridsmith cannot assign what it does not own, and a company cannot waive an individual's moral rights
for them.

**Verdict on the terms.** No clause in Client Terms for Consumers 3.1 or Client Terms for Business
Clients 3.1 is inaccurate or impossible to keep, **provided the documents in §3 are signed before first
use**. So the terms are unchanged on this point. One dependency is easy to miss: Bus 9.2 and Cons 13 ¶6
describe tools and templates "we" own. That is true only once the owner has assigned to the company any
such material the owner created personally or before incorporation (§3, item 1).

## 1. Concepts kept apart

| Concept | Rule | CDPA 1988 | Read at source? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Copyright ownership** (first owner) | The author owns it, unless the author is an employee working in the course of employment, when the employer owns it subject to any agreement to the contrary | s. 11(1)–(2); "employee" = contract of service or apprenticeship (s. 178) | Yes, by B on 6 Oct 2026 (`research/GS-LEGAL-001/B-business-terms.md:53`) |
| **Assignment** | Not effective unless in writing signed by or on behalf of the assignor | s. 90(3) | Yes, by B, G and the citation ledger (`02-CITATION-LEDGER.md:794-807`) |
| **Future-copyright assignment** | A signed agreement assigning future copyright vests it in the assignee when the work is created, if the assignee is then entitled as against everyone else. A payment-conditional assignment is doubtful on that test (B, medium) | s. 91 | Yes (as above) |
| **Moral rights** | Right to be identified (s. 77, only once asserted, s. 78); right to object to derogatory treatment (s. 80); false attribution (s. 84). They cannot be assigned (s. 94) and pass on death (s. 95). They do not apply to computer programs or computer-generated works (ss. 79(2), 81(2)) | ss. 77–95 | ss. 78, 79, 81, 84, 87 read by B; **ss. 77, 80, 82, 94, 95 not read at source** (`PRIMARY SOURCE ACCESS BLOCKED` in R3) |
| **Employee exceptions** | The s. 77 right does not apply to anything done by or with the authority of the copyright owner where copyright first vested in the employer (s. 79(3)). The s. 80 right is qualified for such works (s. 82) | ss. 79(3), 82 | 79(3) yes (B); s. 82 no (search summary only) |
| **Moral-rights waiver** | In writing signed by the person giving up the right. It may cover a specific work, works of a description, or works generally, existing or future, and may be conditional. One made in favour of the owner or prospective owner is presumed to extend to that owner's licensees and successors in title unless a contrary intention appears | s. 87 | Yes (B, G); the presumption's exact wording only as a search summary |
| **Consent** | Consenting to a specific act is different from waiving the right generally | s. 87(1) | As above |
| **Commissioned work** | Commissioning does not make the commissioner the owner. Courts have sometimes implied an equitable assignment (*Griggs v Evans* [2005] EWCA Civ 11), which is fact-specific. **Do not rely on it** | — | Search summary only |
| **Computer-generated work** | Where there is no human author, the author is the person who made the arrangements necessary for its creation. Whether AI-assisted output is protected at all is unsettled | ss. 9(3), 178 | Yes (B); policy position in flux |

## 2. Role by role — what Gridsmith needs

| Role | First owner | What Gridsmith needs | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Employee** | Gridsmith, for work in the course of employment (s. 11(2)) | An IP clause in the employment contract. It acknowledges s. 11(2), assigns related work made outside the course of employment, and includes an s. 87 waiver. The waiver is not strictly needed for in-course work (ss. 79(3), 82) but costs nothing | Covers the edges of "course of employment" and keeps Cons 10.2 literally true |
| **2. Director / officer** | **The director personally**, unless the director is also employed under a contract of service and the work is made in the course of that employment. A directorship is not employment. Fiduciary or trust arguments exist but are not relied on | A signed director-to-company assignment of existing (s. 90(3)) and future (s. 91) copyright, plus an s. 87 waiver | Otherwise the company holds nothing to transfer to the client |
| **3a. Freelancer (individual)** | The freelancer | A subcontract signed **before the work is used**, containing: a present assignment of future copyright (s. 91), **not conditional on payment**; an s. 87 waiver in favour of Gridsmith, its licensees, successors in title and clients; an originality warranty; disclosure of AI tools used and confirmation their terms allow assignment; confidentiality | Cons 13 ¶4; Bus 9.3 ¶3 |
| **3b. Subcontracting company** | The company, if its people are employees or have assigned to it; otherwise the individuals | As 3a, signed by the company, plus a warranty that its own chain is complete and an obligation to deliver an individual waiver (§3, item 5) from each author | A company cannot waive its people's moral rights (s. 87(2)) |
| **4. Commissioned writer (credited)** | The writer | As 3a. The quotation or Scope records the credit; a waiver is needed only so far as the credit arrangement does not cover it | Cons 10.2 "unless your quotation says otherwise" |
| **5. Ghostwriter** | The ghostwriter (or Gridsmith if an employee) | As 3a or 1, plus a signed waiver of both the s. 77 and s. 80 rights, no s. 78 assertion anywhere, and confidentiality (Cons 14 ¶3) | Cons 10.2; Bus 13 |
| **6. The owner personally** | **Unknown.** No repository record says whether the owner is employed by Gridsmith Ltd under a contract of service. If not, the owner owns everything they create. If so, Gridsmith owns in-course work only. Either way, work made before incorporation (24 February 2026) or outside employment stays with the owner | A signed owner-to-company assignment of existing and future copyright, drafted "so far as not already vested under s. 11(2)", covering pre-incorporation and background material and output from AI tools the owner arranges; plus an unconditional s. 87 waiver (works generally, existing and future); a board minute | This is what makes Cons 13 / Bus 9.3 deliverable for work the owner makes, and makes "our own tools, templates…" (Bus 9.2; Cons 13 ¶6) true |
| **7. AI-assisted work** | Human contributions follow rows 1–6. Purely AI-generated output: the person who made the arrangements (s. 9(3)), if protected at all | Cover AI-generated output in the owner, employee and subcontractor assignments; check each AI tool's output terms allow assignment; disclose AI use per Cons 13 / Bus 9.3 | The terms already say protection is not promised |
