# Template 05 — Individual moral-rights waiver

**Status:** internal template (`GS-LEGAL-001-R4`, 7 October 2026). **Not signed.** Not legal advice.
Fill-ins are `[● …]`.

**Use:** signed by **each individual author**: the owner, an employee, an individual freelancer, or a
subcontracting company's personnel. It is the "signed written waiver" that Cons 10.2 and Bus 13 promise
for ghostwritten books, and the waiver Bus 9.3 ¶3 promises "where the Scope requires it". A waiver must
be in writing signed by the person giving up the right (Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988
("CDPA") section 87(2)).

It does **not** transfer copyright. That is templates 01–04.

## Notes

**A — scope.** Section 87 allows a waiver for a specific work, works of a description, or works
generally, existing or future, and conditional or unconditional. The presumption that a waiver in favour
of the owner or prospective owner extends to their licensees and successors in title is recorded only
as a search summary. This form names them expressly, and the client too, so the result does not depend
on the presumption.

**B — what is not waived.** The right against false attribution (section 84) is the author's protection
against being named as the author of something they did not write. It is not waived and does not need
to be: a client publishing a ghostwritten book under the client's own name attributes the book to the
client, with the client's agreement.

**C — the employee case.** For in-course employee work the section 77 right does not apply where
copyright first vested in the employer (section 79(3)), and section 82 qualifies the section 80 right
(search summary only). Signing this form anyway costs nothing.
