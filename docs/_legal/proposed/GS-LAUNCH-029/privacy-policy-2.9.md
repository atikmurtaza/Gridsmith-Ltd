## 1. Who we are

Gridsmith Ltd is the controller of personal data used for our website, enquiries, business administration and client relationships. Our contact details are in section 15. Where we process personal data on a client's behalf for a project, we act under its instructions and agreed data-processing terms; the client remains responsible for its own privacy information.

This policy covers website visitors, people who contact us, clients and their representatives, and review authors whose information we hold.

## 2. Information we collect

Depending on your dealings with us, we collect:

- your name, contact details and organisation;
- enquiry details, including the service, budget, timetable, publishing requirements and any links you provide;
- correspondence, quotations, contracts, invoices and payment records;
- project materials and personal data within them;
- technical request information, such as IP address and browser details, processed by our website hosting and content management providers for delivery and security; and
- if you allow optional analytics, the information described in section 5.

We receive information from you, people acting for you and project participants. A copy of reviews comes from Freelancer (section 2A). Payment providers supply transaction and reconciliation information. The public website has no enquiry form. Contact links let you send an email or WhatsApp message through your chosen service. The website does not send those messages to an enquiry database or trigger automated enquiry notifications. Messages are accessed by the owner or sales department. Direct enquiries are not currently copied into other systems.

Where you work with us through a freelancing platform such as Freelancer.com, your account, project messages, files, payments and reviews are held on that platform under its own terms and privacy notice, and we use them through our account there to provide the work. We do not keep a separate client database. We copy platform records only where the work or the law needs it: for example, files we download to work on, briefs and files shared with delivery agencies (section 6), invoices and payments in our accounting records, and the review copies described in section 2A. Those copies follow section 8. Records that stay on the platform are kept and deleted under the platform's own rules; we cannot delete them from it.

Please do not send special-category information (such as health, religious or political details), criminal-offence information, passwords or payment-card details through website enquiries or linked files. If agreed work requires sensitive personal data, contact us first so we can agree a suitable secure channel and establish the additional lawful conditions and safeguards. Relevance to a project does not by itself authorise its use.

We assess unsolicited sensitive material and restrict, return or remove it as appropriate.

## 2A. Freelancer reviews

Reviews written about us on Freelancer are not shown on this website, and the website does not fetch or refresh them. We hold copies of 11 of them, with their text and star ratings as published on Freelancer and without reviewers' names, to review permission and rights while Freelancer's written permission is outstanding. Earlier development copies of six reviews also include the reviewer's first name or Freelancer username and the type of work. These copies are currently in our website's source-code repository, which is publicly accessible, and in a publicly readable development dataset at our content management provider. If we show reviews in future, we will do so only with that permission, without reviewers' names and with a link to the original, and we will update this policy first. Removing a name does not necessarily make a review anonymous.

If you wrote a review, contact us about access, objection or erasure. Removing our copy does not remove the original on Freelancer.

## 5. Optional website analytics

Optional analytics run only on a version of our website whose cookie request offers them. If the website shows a cookie notice instead, no analytics service is in use and the rest of this section does not apply.

When they are offered and you allow them, we use Google Analytics 4, provided by Google, and PostHog, provided by PostHog, to count the pages visited and how visitors arrive at the website, as statistics. You can allow or refuse each one separately, and both stay off unless you allow them. The [Cookie Policy](/legal/cookies) lists the cookies and browser storage they use and explains how to change your choice.

When a service runs, its provider receives your IP address, which it uses to estimate your approximate location; the address of each page you visit, without any query or fragment; the website you came from; your browser and device type; and a random identifier stored on your device. Google Analytics does not store IP addresses, and we set PostHog to discard them. We use PostHog to record page views. Google Analytics records page views and standard first-visit, session and engagement information. We use neither service for advertising or personalised-advertising features, Google signals, session recording, heatmaps, surveys or automatic capture of what you click, scroll or type. We do not link analytics to your name or contact details. Pseudonymous information is not necessarily anonymous.

Before we switch either service on, we accept its provider's data-processing terms and turn off its data-sharing settings, so that Google and PostHog process this information for us under those terms. Google Analytics deletes user-level and event-level data once it is two months old, in Google's monthly deletion process; aggregated statistics that do not identify you are kept longer. PostHog keeps event data for one year under our plan; whether older data is erased or kept in other storage is governed by PostHog's terms, and you can ask us to have your PostHog records deleted (section 10).

Withdrawing consent stops further collection in every open page of our website and removes the services' cookies and browser storage from your device. It does not erase what a provider has already received. Analytics records are held under the random identifier rather than your name, and withdrawing removes that identifier from your device, so if you want to ask about your records, contact us before you withdraw or tell us the dates and pages of your visits.

We may later use AI-assisted analysis of aggregated analytics reports to support human judgement. Before doing so we will update this policy. Analytics consent does not authorise sending raw visitor, enquiry or project data to an external AI service.

We may disclose information where legally required, to protect legal rights, or in a genuine sale or reorganisation of the business. We do not sell personal data to advertisers or data brokers.

## 7. International transfers

Our website is hosted in the European Economic Area, which is covered by UK adequacy regulations. Company access is restricted to authorised personnel, and the owner reports no current overseas access by company personnel. This does not mean that our email, messaging and other providers and their subprocessors process information only there. Our hosting, content management, analytics and messaging providers and delivery agencies may process or permit access to personal data in other countries. Historical database and email delivery records may also remain subject to international provider processing; their current locations and retention have not yet been verified.

When analytics are switched on, Google may process analytics information in the United States, and PostHog keeps analytics information in its European Union hosting and may permit access from other countries under its data-processing terms. Some of our delivery agencies are in Pakistan, which is not covered by UK adequacy regulations, and some are in the United States, where those regulations cover only organisations certified under the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Others are in European countries; not every European country is covered.

For transfers outside the UK, including overseas access to project files, we require applicable UK adequacy coverage or appropriate safeguards. Our hosting provider's data-processing terms set out the transfer safeguards for its services, and our analytics providers' data-processing terms do the same for theirs. A transfer to a US recipient whose certification under the UK Extension to the Data Privacy Framework covers it relies on the UK's adequacy regulations. Transfers involving other recipients, including any continuing processing of historical records, require applicable adequacy coverage or appropriate safeguards. The specific arrangements for relevant email, messaging, content management and historical-data processing require verification; website hosting in the European Economic Area does not by itself establish those arrangements. For agencies without adequacy coverage, the required route is the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or appropriate EU standard contractual clauses with the UK Addendum, supported by the required data protection test and any additional safeguards. We require the applicable arrangements before access begins.

Payment and professional providers may also process internationally under their applicable terms and duties. Contact us for details or a copy of relevant safeguards, with confidential information redacted where necessary.

## 8. Retention

We keep information only as long as needed for its purpose. The owner manages retention, deletion, backups and privacy requests. The following documented retention schedule remains the basis for those duties; its current applicability to particular historical holdings and providers requires confirmation:

| Records | Retention |
|---|---|
| Enquiries that do not become projects, including email and messaging correspondence and any retained internal notification emails | 12 months from receipt or last contact, whichever is later |
| Identified spam | 30 days |
| Necessary project and contract records, including the final delivered set | Six years after the end of the financial year in which the project ends |
| Accounting and tax records | Six years after the end of the relevant financial year, or longer where tax law requires |
| Signed rights transfers and waivers | While the rights are relied on, then six years |
| Complaints | With the project record, or two years after closure if there is no project |
| Project working files | Return or deletion within 90 days after the project ends unless otherwise agreed |
| Optional analytics records, when switched on | Google Analytics: user-level and event-level data deleted once two months old, in Google's monthly deletion process; aggregated statistics that do not identify you are kept longer. PostHog: event data kept for one year under our plan. Cookies and browser storage on your device: as set out in the Cookie Policy |

Our documented schedule calls for monthly enquiry and spam deletion reviews, annual reviews of long-term records after their periods end, and quarterly working-file clearance checks. These are review duties, not a statement that review cycles or deletions have been completed; deletion is not represented as automated. Agreed client data-processing instructions also apply. A documented legal hold may extend retention of relevant records, with restricted access and further review.

We retain only proportionate evidence of rights requests and preferences for accountability, disputes and respecting objections. We do not keep records of individual cookie choices; we keep dated versions of our cookie request and policies to show how consent is asked for. The documented schedule requires the Freelancer review copy to be reviewed monthly and when permission, removal requests or source changes require it, and removed when no longer needed or lawful. This does not assert that a scheduled review or removal has already occurred.

Providers may retain technical, security and backup records under their own operational and legal retention rules. We have not verified the currently applicable periods for historical database and email delivery records, other provider logs or backups, or developer-created backup copies. The owner has not established a new company backup system; this does not mean that provider-managed backups are absent, and no tested backup/restore system is claimed. Deleting a main record does not immediately erase every backup; copies awaiting expiry must remain protected and unavailable for ordinary use, and applicable deletions must be reapplied after restoration. Contact us about a particular record or retention period.

## 10. Your rights

Subject to the law, you can request access, correction, erasure, restriction and portability of personal data, object to processing based on legitimate interests, and withdraw consent. **You can object to direct marketing at any time, and we will stop.**

Contact us using section 15. We may need to confirm your identity. We respond without undue delay and normally within one month, explaining any lawful extension or pause. Erasure is subject to exceptions, including necessary legal duties or claims; we explain any refusal and your complaint rights. Requests may involve our processors as well as our own records. Withdrawal stops future consent-based processing without affecting its previous lawfulness; retaining existing personal data still requires a lawful basis.
