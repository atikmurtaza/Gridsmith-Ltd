## 1. Method and limits (read before relying on anything below)

- **Retrieval:** all sources retrieved 6 Oct 2026 with WebSearch/WebFetch.
- **How text was read:** for 8 of the 10 sources the page was read through an automated
  fetch-and-summarise step, so the extraction is a machine paraphrase of the page, not a line-by-line
  reading. For **Brandwave** and **MS InfoTech** the PDF was downloaded and the full text was
  extracted and read directly; clause numbers for those two are exact. Treat the other eight as
  **medium confidence**: a term reported as "not stated" may exist on a linked page the fetch did not reach.
- **Search was cut short.** The web-search quota ran out partway through the work. These were
  searched for and **not obtained**:
  - a UK CAD/technical-drafting firm with published terms. The only drafting result, ADW Drafting
    Services, is in California and is **excluded**;
  - a dedicated UK audiobook producer (The Audio Suite is a general audio post-production studio);
  - a UK self-publishing services company with full published terms. Troubador/Matador's terms URL
    returned 404, and SilverWood's terms were not found.

  These gaps are recorded, not filled by guessing.
- **Falcon Books Publishing (S10) is low-reliability.** Its live page shows unfilled template
  placeholders: the legal name appears as a bracketed placeholder, and the liability-cap period is
  a bracketed number. It is kept only as a data point and as a warning (§5, R18).
- **Excluded as non-comparable:**
  - publishing.co.uk "Author Legal Pack": a template product, not a service;
  - Reedsy Studio terms: these govern platform software, and the marketplace terms URL returned 404;
  - Sprintlaw articles: law-firm marketing, not a business's own terms.

### 3.3 Cross-cutting observations

1. **Consent-based portfolio use is the exception.** Three of the ten sources (S4, S6, S8) grant
   themselves automatic portfolio or publicity rights. Only S1 is clearly consent-based. Gridsmith's
   owner policy puts it in the minority, and that is a positioning point, not a weakness.
2. **"On full payment" assignment is the near-universal IP pattern** (S1, S3, S4, S5, S6, S8, S9).
   Several sources carve out source code (S3, S6) or keep all project files (S7). Gridsmith's
   transfer of bespoke deliverables is more generous than most and consistent with Non-negotiable 6.
3. **Deposit forfeiture is the dominant cancellation model** (S1, S2, S3, S5, S6). Gridsmith's
   "work performed plus committed cost" model (MSA §6; Consumer §7) is the more defensible one.
   Do not trade it for the market norm (§5).
4. **Dormant-project mechanics are common** (S2, S5, S6, S8). Gridsmith has none (§4, G3).
5. **Only one source discloses AI use** (S1). That disclosure is specific: no upload of client text
   to public generative AI, and human review of all output.
6. **Consumer cancellation is handled badly across the market.** Sources either omit it (S1, S3, S5),
   misstate the period (S2's "working days"), or treat it as a refund promise (S10). Gridsmith's
   Consumer §6 is already more careful than any source reviewed.
7. **No source reviewed has a written complaints procedure.** Gridsmith Consumer §13 is ahead.

| R5 | **Cancellation charge on work never performed** (50% of the residual, or up to 100% of the fee) | S8 cl. 8(b), S1, S7 | It charges for work not done. That is the opposite of MSA §6.2's "not a cancellation penalty" principle, risks being a penalty in B2B and is likely unfair for consumers. If capacity reservation is a real cost, it must be priced openly in the Scope and tested by A/B. |
| R6 | **Inactivity triggers forfeiture or makes the whole balance due** | S5 (20 working days), S6 (28 days) | Penalty and unfairness risk. Use a notice-then-close mechanism that charges for work done (G3). |
| R7 | **Flat percentage late fees or admin surcharges** (5% per 28 days, 2% per month, per-missed-instalment fee) | S3, S4, S6 | For B2B the statute already gives a substantial remedy; a contractual rate would need to be one A/B accept as substantial and not a penalty. For consumers, disproportionate default charges are on the potentially unfair list. Keep MSA §5's statutory reservation; no consumer late fee without A/B clearance. |
| R8 | **Short deemed acceptance that also ends defect rights** (7 days; 14 days then all amendments charged; one month for software) | S8 cl. 1(d), 10(d); S9 cl. 6.6 | It turns silence into a waiver. For consumers it cannot remove statutory rights (A/B: CRA s. 57). MSA §8's "evidence of acceptance, latent defects preserved" approach is better and should stay. |
| R9 | **Unilateral price or terms changes** (rates raised at sole discretion; terms changed by email; post-suspension price review; prices changed "prospectively") | S9 cl. 13.2, S8 cl. 8(h), S4, S10 | It undermines the agreed-quote model in the brief. Unilateral variation is a grey-list consumer term (A/B). Any change should go through a Change Order or an agreed review mechanism. |
| R10 | **Blanket exclusion of all delay "whatever the cause"** | S3 | Excludes liability for Gridsmith's own fault. Fails reasonableness in B2B and is ineffective against consumers (A/B). |
| R11 | **Liability cap tied to insurance recoveries or the insured sum** | S8 cl. 12(a), S9 cl. 14.6 | Gridsmith carries no PI cover for Technical work and must never advertise insured or uninsured status (`GS-O005` closure). An insurance-referenced cap implies cover. |
| R12 | **Keeping source code, project files or masters, or locking the client into agency hosting** | S3, S6, S7 | Contradicts Non-negotiable 6 (claim no more than the contract gives, and Digital's ownership module). Lock-in for consumers is a fairness risk. |
| R13 | **Non-disparagement** | S6 | Risky against consumers, because it may be seen as suppressing reviews or complaints (A/B to check current consumer-protection law on reviews). Of little value B2B. |
| R14 | **12-month non-solicitation of staff and subcontractors** | S8 | Restraint-of-trade risk, poor fit for a small studio using specialists, unsuitable for consumers. |
| R15 | **Overpayments not reclaimable after 12 months** | S8 | No commercial justification; the money belongs to the client. |
| R16 | **12-month contractual bar on claims, for consumers** | S4 | Cuts the statutory limitation period; likely unfair for consumers. B2B only, if at all (G27). |
| R17 | **Restating the cooling-off period wrongly** ("14 working days") or describing it as a refund policy | S2, S10 | Mis-stating a statutory right, even generously, is a misleading-information risk. It also contradicts the brief ("not a blanket 14-day refund policy"). Track the regulations' own wording, verified by A/B. |
| R18 | **Publishing terms with unfilled template placeholders** | S10 | Shows how easily this happens. Gridsmith's launch gates (`check:launch`, legal parity) should keep refusing `[TK]`-style markers in served legal text. |
| R19 | **Uncapped indemnities from consumers** | S2, S6 (pattern) | See G18. For consumers, use a content-responsibility statement and a right to decline, not an indemnity, unless A/B clear one. |

## 6. Reminders for whoever drafts next

1. **Competitor terms are not authority.** Several clauses above are probably unlawful against
   consumers or unenforceable as penalties. That something is common in the market proves nothing
   about whether it is lawful.
2. **Every proposed Gridsmith clause needs its own primary-law check** by Agents A/B, against
   current legislation.gov.uk text with commencement checked. That covers at least:
   - consumer cancellation, express request and acknowledgement;
   - fairness and transparency under CRA Part 2;
   - statutory rights for services under CRA ss. 49–57;
   - reasonableness under UCTA;
   - late-payment interest;
   - consumer-credit treatment of instalments;
   - subscription and rolling-contract rules;
   - performers' and moral rights;
   - data-processing terms;
   - ADR information duties.
3. **Do not import competitor numbers.** Every percentage and period in §3 (deposit sizes, 28-day
   dormancy, 30/60-day warranties, 12-month limits) is a data point. The brief forbids fixed
   percentages or schedules as policy; payment structure lives in each Scope.
4. **Separate business and consumer outcomes.** Several patterns (indemnities, contractual claim
   limits, late fees, deemed acceptance) may be acceptable B2B and unacceptable B2C.
5. **Nothing here supports "solicitor approved", "fully compliant" or similar claims.**
6. **Re-verify sources before quoting this benchmark elsewhere.** Competitor pages change, and eight
   of the ten were read through automated summarisation (§1).
