# International agency and provider transfer clearance

22 October 2026. Owner-confirmed agencies: UK, USA, Pakistan and Europe. Exact agency entities, European countries, access histories, signatures and onward processors remain unverified. These are execution facts to record before access, not another question needed to draft the seven policies. Review existing agreements first; no transfer agreement was executed by this task.

1. Identify exporter, exact importer entity/address, controller/processor/sub-processor roles, data, purposes, necessary access, storage and every access/onward country. Data minimisation or encryption does not automatically anonymise data or make a transfer lawful. A separate overseas recipient's remote access can be a restricted transfer even on UK-hosted files.
2. For UK-only work, confirm no overseas access/onward transfer. UK GDPR contracts, confidentiality and IP protection still apply. For an EEA or other adequate country, retain dated evidence of applicable adequacy and scope. “Europe” is insufficient; check each country and onward route.
3. For a US recipient, verify the exact legal entity's active certification, UK Extension and data/scope coverage on the official DPF register. Keep evidence and recheck when the arrangement changes. Parent registration or EU-only coverage is insufficient. If not covered, use another valid mechanism.
4. Pakistan has no general UK adequacy arrangement. For applicable routine transfers to Pakistan, non-adequate Europe or an uncovered US agency, normally execute the official UK IDTA or the correct EU SCC module plus UK Addendum, complete all tables/annexes and a documented transfer risk assessment/data protection test, with proportionate supplementary measures. Determine the appropriate module from the actual roles. Routine outsourcing must not rely casually on Article 49 consent/contract exceptions.
5. Use official mandatory wording unchanged except as expressly permitted. Link the agency instructions, duration, data/sub-data-subject categories, security, technical support, onward restrictions and contact/complaint details. Complete and sign as required; a drafted generic transfer paragraph or NDA is insufficient. Reassess practical destination-law risks and access measures; if adequate safeguards cannot be achieved, withhold that access.
6. Keep the Article 28 agreement and client authorisation separate from the transfer test: both may be necessary. Flow down duties, no unapproved sub-processing or locations, breach/rights assistance and return/deletion. Signed IP assignments and actual-creator consents/waivers protect the promised client rights independently of privacy terms.
7. Evidence record per recipient: entity/countries, controller/roles, minimal data, signed agreement/version/date, authorised access, client permission, adequacy/DPF check or instrument/annexes/test, security measures, review date, actual access logs, return/deletion record and operator. Flag previous access whose contracts/safeguards cannot be demonstrated for correction; prospective policy wording does not validate it.
