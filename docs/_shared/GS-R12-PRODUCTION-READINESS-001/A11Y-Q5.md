and takes the 98th-percentile luminance over each text box as the background.

Both retained PNGs (hashes verified in `GS-R12-CAPTURE-VALIDITY-001`) contradict their own
same-capture DOM receipts:

- **Mobile:** the PNG paints the carousel's previous/play/next controls and the gold pause
  underline at y≈213–240, inside the caption box, and paints them **gold**. The DOM records put
  those controls at y≈959–992 (below the 844px viewport) with **transparent** stroke/fill under the
  mask. I inspected the PNG directly: the controls are visibly unmasked and displaced ~740px, while
  the review ring below them is drawn in place.
- **Desktop:** the PNG paints the Context links ("How an engagement works…", "About Gridsmith")
  inside the heading sample; the DOM puts them at y=−720 (above the viewport), transparent.

A frame that shows elements both **unmasked** (pre-mask paint) and **displaced** (another scroll
offset) while other layers are current is a composited frame assembled from stale and fresh
content. That is a capture/compositor disagreement. It is **not** evidence of a caption or heading
contrast defect a visitor would see, and it is **not** evidence that the verifier's arithmetic is
wrong: given that bitmap, the ratios are correct.

### Root-cause hypotheses (ranked by fit to the evidence)

1. **Stale raster tiles under CPU/raster starvation on the shared runner** — SwiftShader rasterising
   a WebGL scene plus a 3D-transformed review ring on a small VM; `Page.captureScreenshot` with
   `fromSurface: true` returns the last composited surface, which can still hold old tiles for some
   layers. Fits: unmasked (pre-mask) content, displaced position, other layers current, never seen
   on faster local hardware even with CPU throttle (throttle slows the main thread, not raster
   threads).
2. Impl-thread scroll/compositor offset not yet committed for one layer. Fits displacement, fits
   unmasked content less well.
3. Font swap or animation state. Poor fit: DOM records equal before/after; fonts `loaded`; paused.
